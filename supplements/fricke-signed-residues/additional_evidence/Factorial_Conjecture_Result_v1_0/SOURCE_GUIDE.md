# Sources and their roles

Included PDFs are user-supplied copies; read the actual statements and verify printed pagination.

- Robert (1980), Congruences entre séries d’Eisenstein, dans le cas supersingulier, Inventiones Mathematicae 61, 103–158. The manuscript uses Théorème B and §3.3, Lemma 7, equation (19), for isogeny transformation of E_(p+1). Inspect for stronger scalar evaluations.
- Landweber (1988), Supersingular elliptic curves and congruences for Legendre polynomials, LNM 1326, 69–93. The manuscript points to Theorem 3 and §4 for half-exponent discriminant identities.
- Kaneko & Zagier (1998), Supersingular j-invariants, hypergeometric series, and Atkin’s orthogonal polynomials, AMS/IP Studies in Advanced Mathematics 7, 97–126. Inspect hypergeometric and polynomial formulas as well as §10.

Essential source not included as a separate PDF:
- Baker (1998), A supersingular congruence for modular forms, Acta Arithmetica 86(1), 91–100. Author copy: https://www.maths.gla.ac.uk/~ajb/dvi-ps/modforms.pdf . DOI: https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-86-1-91-100 . Read the precise signed supersingular congruence, not only its square.

Other leads, not assertions of relevance:
- Ono, Modular forms for chromatic homotopy: supersingular congruences, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16175 . Check the version used; inspect Remarks concerning the half exponent.
- Classical Gauss-factorial, Jacobi-sum, Hasse-invariant and Legendre-polynomial evaluations. Test exact applicability and all residue classes before claiming a precedent.
